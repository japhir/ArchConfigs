#+title: 0004 — SCHEDULED means "resurface on this date" only; calendar is the hard landscape

* Status
accepted

* Decision

=SCHEDULED= carries exactly one meaning: hide this item until the given date,
then resurface it (a tickler date). It never means "I'd like to work on this
around now." The hard landscape — things that genuinely must happen on a
specific date — is carried only by =DEADLINE= and timed events in =gcal.org=.
This is why =org-agenda-todo-ignore-scheduled= is =t=: scheduled-but-not-yet-due
items are deliberately kept out of the next-actions lists until their date.

* Why

Soft scheduling ("do around now") breeds a pile of overdue items that the
agenda nags about, which erodes trust in the system — the cardinal GTD failure.
Reserving =SCHEDULED= for the tickler keeps the calendar a trustworthy hard
landscape and the next-actions lists honest.

=TICK= and =NEXT= + =SCHEDULED= are *not* equivalent: the =n= view filters on
the =NEXT= keyword, so a =TICK= item never appears there regardless of any
date, whereas =NEXT= is the active "do this now" state. =TICK= is a distinct
parked state (review-this-later); =SCHEDULED= is the orthogonal mechanism that
sets *when* something resurfaces.

* Consequences

- Items you "want to do soon" stay =NEXT= (chosen during review/engage), not
  =SCHEDULED= to a wished date.
- =org-agenda-todo-ignore-scheduled t= must stay; flipping it would reintroduce
  scheduled items into the todo lists and break the convention.
